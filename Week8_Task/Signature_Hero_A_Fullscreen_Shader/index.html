Behind the hero

One fragment shader, nine small ideas

The background above is a single GLSL program that runs once for every pixel, every frame. It only knows three things: where the pixel is, what time it is, and where your cursor is.

  1. Coordinates

    u_resolution turns a pixel into a 0–1 position, then into centred, aspect-correct space so nothing stretches on a phone.

  2. Silk from noise

    Five layers of value noise, fed back into themselves twice (domain warping). That feedback is what makes the folds look like fabric.

  3. Time

    u_time slides the two warp layers in opposite directions, very slowly. It reads as rolling, not scrolling.

  4. Cursor lean

    u_mouse bends space toward your pointer with a soft falloff, so the silk is tugged rather than painted on. JavaScript eases it so it never jerks.

  5. Readable by design

    A dark veil is baked into the shader right where the headline sits, plus a vignette and a pinch of film grain to stop colour banding.

  6. Polite to your device

    Pixel ratio capped at 1.5, resolution drops if frames get slow, and it stops drawing when the tab is hidden, the hero is off-screen, or you ask for reduced motion.
